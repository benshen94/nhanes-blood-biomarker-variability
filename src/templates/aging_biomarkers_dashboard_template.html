Personal calculator

Try the open blood-age model.

This tab implements the published PhenoAge blood-chemistry equation using age plus nine routine lab markers. It runs locally in your browser and never uploads your values from this page.

What it is

PhenoAge, built from NHANES blood chemistry

PhenoAge is an open blood-based model from Morgan Levine and colleagues. It maps your age and nine routine biomarkers to the age in a reference U.S. population that had a similar mortality-associated blood profile.

The blood model was developed in adults from NHANES III and later evaluated in NHANES IV. It is best understood as a research-oriented summary of blood chemistry and hematology, not a diagnosis.

Caveats

Useful context, not personal destiny.

  • PhenoAge was calibrated on U.S. adults in NHANES, so it is a population-reference model rather than a direct measure of how fast your body is aging internally.
  • Short-term inflammation, infection, recent hard training, medications, dehydration, and lab-method differences can move the result.
  • This tab is for adults and should not be used as a diagnosis, a screening decision, or a substitute for medical advice.
  • If your CRP report uses a lower-limit symbol such as <0.1, this calculator still needs a positive numeric value because the published equation uses log(CRP).

Methods in plain language

What this dashboard is and what it is not.

This public dashboard focuses on healthy-adult cross-sectional patterns in NHANES blood biomarkers. It is meant for exploration and intuition building, not for clinical decision-making.

The default public view uses the 10th to 90th percentile band within each age bin before computing summary statistics. That makes the overall patterns easier to read and less sensitive to extreme outliers.

  • Age bins run from 20-24 through 80-84. Ages 85 and older are excluded from the age-bin trend summaries.
  • The healthy cohort excludes pregnancy, diagnosed diabetes, diagnosed cardiovascular disease, cancer history, kidney failure indicators, and liver disease history.
  • The charts are cross-sectional. They compare different people at different ages, not the same people followed over time.
  • Sex divergence shows whether female and male median curves move apart after each is normalized to its own 20-24 baseline.
Why some views matter

Mean shifts do not tell the whole story.

A biomarker can keep a similar median while becoming much wider, or it can develop a longer upper tail while the center barely moves. That is why this dashboard foregrounds medians, spread, and tail shape separately.
